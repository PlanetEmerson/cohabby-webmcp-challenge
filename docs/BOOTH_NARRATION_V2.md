# CoHabby Living booth narration V2

Read only the text under each take. The labels and notes are not spoken.

Delivery: confident, warm, and conversational. Speak at your normal pace. Say “Web M-C-P” clearly. Leave one clean breath between takes.

## Take 1 · 0:00 to 0:14

Finding a room is one thing. Finding someone you can actually live well with is harder. I built CoHabby Living to put roommate compatibility first.

## Take 2 · 0:14 to 0:31

The page gives a browser agent six small WebMCP tools. They read the same state I see, five can change this page in real time, and each has one clear job. There is no chatbot behind it and no second agent.

## Take 3 · 0:31 to 0:50

I start with a few everyday habits: budget, timing, pets, quiet time, and how I want shared space to feel. The agent can read these choices and stage a living plan, but only I can approve it.

## Take 4 · 0:50 to 1:10

Once I approve, the agent finds fictional people and homes that fit those needs. The person comes first. The room is useful context. I can select a whole card, or ask CoHabby why that roommate may fit.

## Take 5 · 1:10 to 1:32

This Synergy Lens is the signature WebMCP moment. It opens from a live match and explains three practical reasons our household rhythms may work. This challenge uses demo scores.

## Take 6 · 1:32 to 1:54

Next, the agent compares two current roommates side by side. I can scan the Synergy read, rent, move timing, daily rhythm, and household boundaries. Then I choose who to greet and whether the note should sound warm, direct, or casual.

## Take 7 · 1:54 to 2:17

The final tool prepares a first hello using only facts already visible on the page. I can edit every word. The agent cannot send it or confirm it. That last click stays mine, and the demo says clearly that no real message was sent.

## Take 8 · 2:17 to 2:38

Under the hood, every tool has a strict schema, current-state checks, cancellation, safe output validation, and a render acknowledgement. The source, assets, and tests are public, and the live footer identifies the exact deployed revision.

## Take 9 · 2:38 to 2:53

CoHabby Living turns everyday habits into a thoughtful roommate introduction on one shared page. The browser handles the structured work. I keep the human decisions. That is the kind of web I want to build.

## Booth checklist

- Record one continuous safety read first.
- Then record the nine takes separately.
- If a take misses, pause and restart that take from the beginning.
- Leave two seconds of room tone at the beginning and end.
- Export the continuous read and each take as 48 kHz WAV files.
